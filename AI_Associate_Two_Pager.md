## The Problem (It's Worse Than You Think)

The average analyst spends **60-70% of their time** on work that doesn't require human judgment:

```
Parsing decks             → 30-45 min each
Writing call summaries    → 30-60 min each
Updating the CRM          → Constant friction
Drafting IC memos         → 3-5 hours each
Chasing missing info      → Endless email threads
```

Meanwhile, partners make investment decisions based on:
- Context scattered across email, Slack, Notion, and people's heads
- Memos that are already outdated by the time they're read
- "I think the founder said..." rather than "The founder said, at 23:45..."

**The result?** Slower decisions. Missed signals. Deals that fall through cracks. Good companies that never get a fair look.

## Questions We Get

**"How is this different from Affinity / CRMs with AI features?"**

Affinity is a database that added AI features. We're an AI that happens to keep records. The difference: Affinity waits for you to ask. Our AI shows up Monday morning with your weekend decks already processed, scored, and researched. It drafts the memo before you ask. It notices the revenue discrepancy you would have missed. *It works for you, not the other way around.*

**"What about data security?"**

Non-negotiable. Fund data is logically isolated, encrypted at rest and in transit, never shared across funds, never used to train models for other customers. We're pursuing SOC 2 Type II certification before going to market.

**"Does this replace analysts?"**

No—it *unleashes* them. Your analyst stops spending 4 hours writing an IC memo and starts spending 45 minutes making it better. They stop parsing decks and start evaluating opportunities. The grunt work disappears; the judgment work remains. *That's the work they wanted to do anyway.*

**"How does it learn our fund's preferences?"**

Two ways: (1) Explicit feedback—you tell it when something's wrong, and it remembers. (2) Implicit signals—it notices what you accept, edit, or discard. Correct a fit score once? It adjusts. Edit out a memo section repeatedly? It stops including it. *The more you use it, the more it feels like it's been at your fund for years.*
